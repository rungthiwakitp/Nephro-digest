### Abstract

Herpes zoster (HZ), the reactivation of latent varicella-zoster virus (VZV), remains one of the most frequent opportunistic infections in solid organ transplant (SOT) recipients. In this population, HZ is associated with a high burden of disease, including increased rates of postherpetic neuralgia and disseminated disease. Despite universal antiviral prophylaxis with valaciclovir or valganciclovir, HZ can occur after discontinuation of antivirals at a yearly rate of 2-3%, and the risk rises further during intensified immunosuppression.

### Abstract

We congratulate Shemesh et al1 for their very informative randomized study of telemetric intervention (TI) (N=72) versus standard-of-care (SOC) (N=76) in evaluating biopsy-proven acute rejection (BPAR) during a 2-year follow-up of high-risk liver transplant adolescents, i.e., those having a 2-year pre-enrollment medication level variability index (MLVI) >2units (SD of tacrolimus levels >2). The results suggest that TI (using repeated video/audio calls between interventionists, patients, and parents) helps in reducing BPAR occurrence as well as in achieving an MLVI < 2units.
