### Abstract

Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome (INS) is the most common chronic glomerular disease in children. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, and the initial response, differentiating steroid-sensitive (SSNS) from steroid-resistant (SRNS) forms, is the strongest prognostic factor. While most childhood-onset INS results from an incompletely defined dysregulation of the immune system, up to one-third of children with initial SRNS have a monogenic etiology. SSNS typically follows a relapsing–remitting course, with one-half of the patients having frequent relapses or steroid-dependence (FRNS/SDNS).

### Abstract

A 36-year-old male with end stage kidney disease secondary to C3 glomerulonephritis (C3G) status-post living-unrelated kidney transplantation complicated by recurrent C3G one month after transplant underwent a protocol biopsy for a clinical trial of pegcetacoplan. The biopsy was performed three years after transplant and two years after commencing treatment with pegcetacoplan. Laboratory evaluation showed serum creatinine 2.99 mg/dl (baseline serum creatinine 1.3-1.75 mg/dl), urine protein to creatinine ratio 193 mg/g, and serum albumin 4.8 g/dl.
