### Abstract

Human kidney organoids provide genetically tractable models of polycystic kidney disease, but variable cyst formation and limited scalability have constrained their use in drug discovery. Vanichapol et al. describe a scalable suspension culture of ureteric epithelium–like organoids in which PKD2 loss drives rapid, spontaneous, and highly penetrant cystogenesis. The study advances organoid-based drug screening for polycystic kidney disease, identifies Toll-like receptor 4 signaling as a candidate regulator of cyst growth, and highlights how epithelial identity and maturation state shape drug responses.

### Abstract

There are sex/gender differences in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) and chronic kidney disease epidemiology that may be due to biological differences in sex hormones. In the Renal Iohexol Clearance Survey, a prospective observational study in which iohexol plasma clearance measured GFR was assessed longitudinally, testosterone was associated with baseline measured GFR but not measured GFR decline in multivariable regression models. This suggests that endogenous testosterone is not a driver of age-related GFR decline in healthy middle-aged cisgender males.

### Abstract

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), the most common subtype of RCC, is characterized by constitutive activation of hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF-1α and HIF-2α), with HIF-2α promoting peroxisome turnover. Although peroxisomes play key roles in lipid metabolism and reactive oxygen species homeostasis, they remain relatively understudied in cancer research, and their contribution to ccRCC is poorly understood. Notably, peroxisome abundance is more frequently reduced in low-grade than in high-grade tumors, suggesting a potential role in tumorigenesis.
