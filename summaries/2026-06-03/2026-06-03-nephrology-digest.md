### Abstract

This case demonstrates that persistent isolated proteinuria, maintained for over a decade with entirely normal serum albumin and kidney function, can be the hallmark of a renal-limited cubilin-amnionless (CUBN-AMN) pathway defect rather than a primary podocytopathy. The patient’s clinical journey began in infancy, when proteinuria was incidentally detected (Figure 1a). Initial urine protein-to-creatinine ratios were approximately 230 mg/mmol (approximately 2 g/g Cr), subsequently stabilizing between 55 and 110 mg/mmol (between 0.5 and 1.0 g/g Cr).

- Matched keywords: kidney, proteinuria

### Abstract

A 23-year-old man presented with edema, nephrotic range proteinuria (12 g/24 hours), serum albumin 2.2 g/dL and creatinine 1.3 mg/dL. Testing for Hepatitis B, C, HIV, dsDNA, C3, C4 was unrevealing (Supplementary Table S1). A kidney biopsy showed membranous nephropathy (MN) with “full house” staining for IgG, IgM, IgA, C3 and C1q, but without staining for PLA2R, THSD7A, NELL1 or exostosin 2 (Figure 1), interpreted as membranous (class V) lupus nephritis. Treatment with prednisone 60 mg/d, hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) 400 mg/d and mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) coincided with normalization of albumin (4.4 g/dL) and creatinine (0.7 mg/dL) (Supplementary Figure S1).
