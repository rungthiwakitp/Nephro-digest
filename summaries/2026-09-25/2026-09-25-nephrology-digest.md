### Abstract

Normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) is emerging as more than a preservation strategy, offering a platform for graft assessment and ex situ intervention.1 To fully exploit this opportunity, biomarkers are needed that reflect ongoing graft injury and predict subsequent recovery. Complement is particularly attractive in this setting because ischemia–reperfusion activates the cascade and several complement components can be produced locally within the kidney. The published data on complement activation during kidney NMP is limited and previously reported in discarded human kidneys.
