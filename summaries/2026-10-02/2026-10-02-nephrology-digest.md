### Abstract

Coffee, one of the most widely consumed beverages worldwide, contains caffeine, chemically known as 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine. Caffeine is metabolized by CYP1A2 in the liver to paraxanthine, theophylline and theobromine and then subsequently converted by xanthine oxidase into 1-methyluric acid (1-MUA), the main caffeine derivative found in urine (Supplementary Figure S1). We report the case of a 60-year-old Caucasian woman, active smoker, with a history of rheumatoid arthritis, heart disease and esophageal leiomyoma, who presented with flank pain due to multiple kidney stones.
