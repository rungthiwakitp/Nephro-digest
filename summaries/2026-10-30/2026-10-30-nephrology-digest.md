- Matched keywords: kidney

### Abstract

Alloreactivity after kidney transplantation occurs via both memory responses resulting from prior sensitization and de novo responses that develop due to donor–recipient disparity for the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) complex. HLA compatibility (absence of HLA-DSA) and the degree of HLA matching inform on the likelihood of allorecognition that can cause allograft rejection and allograft loss, and are therefore the two pillars of alloimmune risk assessment. Over the past decades, the detection of anti-HLA antibodies has become more sensitive and specific through single antigen bead testing, and advances in HLA typing from serology to DNA level have been instrumental for more detailed analysis of HLA compatibility.

### Abstract

Normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) is a promising platform for assessment and treatment of kidneys prior to transplantation. Initial cool acellular perfusion with a graduated transition to normothermia might offer advantages over direct NMP reperfusion by 1) reducing ischaemia reperfusion injury, and 2) flushing donor-derived blood components from the microvasculature prior to the addition of red cells. 8 pairs of porcine kidneys were assigned randomly to up to 24 hours of NMP, or to an adapted protocol including initial sub-normothermic acellular (SNAP) and transition phases prior to NMP.
