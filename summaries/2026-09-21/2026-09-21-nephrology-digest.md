### Abstract

Sex influences susceptibility, recovery, and long-term outcomes after acute kidney injury (AKI) in humans. Rodent models have been invaluable for elucidating AKI mechanisms. However, most studies have focused on males, assuming direct applicability to females, an assumption that remains largely untested. In males, the transcription factor SOX9 regulates injury-associated proximal tubule cell states, promoting adaptive repair and survival in the early phase while contributing to fibrosis in later phases.
