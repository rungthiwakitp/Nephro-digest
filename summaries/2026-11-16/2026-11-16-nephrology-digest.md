### Abstract

A 33-year-old woman was referred for ureteral stenting before hysterectomy for a suspected 14-cm uterine sarcoma. An outside computed tomography (CT) report described a solitary right kidney. She had no urinary symptoms, and serum creatinine was 0.8 mg/dl. Cystoscopy showed two normal ureteral orifices, with efflux only from the right. Ultrasonography showed a large right kidney, interpreted as compensatory hypertrophy. Renal agenesis was presumed, and only the right ureter was stented. At surgery, the left ureter, embedded in parametrial fibrosis, was divided on the assumption that it drained nothing; brisk urine flow followed, and the ureter was reimplanted.
