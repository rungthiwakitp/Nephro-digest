- Matched keywords: kidney, renal

### Abstract

A 3-month-old female infant, born at 28 weeks of gestation, presented with fever and hematochezia due to Candida enteritis. Blood and urine cultures grew Candida albicans. Renal ultrasonography revealed a left renal abscess and a hyperechoic fungal ball impacted at the ureteropelvic junction, causing acute obstruction (Figure 1A). A left percutaneous nephrostomy was performed, followed by intravenous fluconazole and local amphotericin B irrigation. Seventeen days later, identical sonographic findings developed in the contralateral kidney, with a newly formed fungal ball obstructing the right ureteropelvic junction (Figure 1B).

### Abstract

A 51-year-old man had been on peritoneal dialysis (PD) for 9 years, a period complicated by several episodes of peritonitis. Eight months prior to presentation, an episode of fungal peritonitis necessitated PD catheter removal, prompting a switch to maintenance hemodialysis along with concurrent antifungal therapy. One month before admission, he developed abdominal pain, distension, vomiting, and obstipation. His symptoms persisted despite gastrointestinal decompression, abdominal paracentesis and drainage, antifungal therapy, and hemodialysis at an outside hospital.
