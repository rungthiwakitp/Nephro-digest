### Abstract

Kidney outcome trials demonstrate that sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors consistently slow chronic kidney disease progression; however, the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely defined, as benefits extend beyond glycemic control and hemodynamic effects across diabetic and non-diabetic chronic kidney disease. This review advances a unifying framework in which metabolic unloading of the proximal tubule contributes to renoprotection. The metabolic burden of solute reabsorption places the proximal tubule at the center of renal energetics, predisposing it to hypoxia, mitochondrial stress, and maladaptive injury responses.

### Abstract

Efforts to improve access to kidney transplant are limited by an incomplete view on the full arc of the evaluation process. We performed a retrospective cohort study among 39,691 adults receiving care within the US Veterans Affairs Health Care System (VA) with incident advanced kidney disease (eGFR≤20 mL/min/1.73 m2) between 10/1/2011 and 12/31/2018. We used sequence analysis to describe sequential steps in the transplant process over 5 years of follow-up. Cohort members’ median age at cohort entry was 70.1 (IQR 63.9, 80.2) years and 97.2% were men.
