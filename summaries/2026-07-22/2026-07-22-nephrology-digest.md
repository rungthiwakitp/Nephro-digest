Numerous studies have suggested a link between disturbances of mineral metabolism and impaired erythropoiesis or resistance to erythropoietin action in CKD. ### Abstract

Liver transplantation for acute alcohol-associated hepatitis has increased sharply over the past decade, and these recipients are young, with excellent post-transplant survival.1 Over the same period, the 2017 simultaneous liver–kidney allocation policy established a “safety net” that grants liver recipients with persistent renal failure priority for a kidney transplant between 60 and 365 days after transplant.2 In this issue, Fleischer and colleagues ask how often this growing population uses that net, and report that recipients transplanted for acute alcohol-associated hepatitis were roughly twice as likely to be listed for (10.2% vs 4.8%) and to receive (6.2% vs 3.2%) a safety-net kidney as balanced controls.
