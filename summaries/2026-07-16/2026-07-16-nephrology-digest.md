### Abstract

The number of donation after circulatory death (DCD) donors continues to increase worldwide and, in some countries, now exceeds those of donation after brain death (DBD) donors. Early DCD retrieval practice largely relied upon in-situ rapid cold perfusion (RCP) followed by static cold storage (SCS), and in general excellent transplant results were achieved with this approach. Organ utilisation from DCD donors remains, however, poorer than from DBD donors, particularly for DCD liver grafts. This reflects the susceptibility of the biliary system to warm ischaemic damage, with approximately 20% of recipients of RCP DCD livers developing clinically significant ischaemic cholangiopathy (IC), and with graft and patient survival poorer than for comparable DBD livers 1.
