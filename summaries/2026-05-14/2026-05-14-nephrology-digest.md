### Abstract

Urinary single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) provides a noninvasive and mechanistically informative approach for studying kidney allograft biology. We established a high-quality urinary scRNA-seq workflow and applied it to 12 kidney recipients with various clinical states. Benchmarking genotype-based deconvolution against published tissue datasets confirmed accurate assignment of donor and recipient cell origins in urine, despite the low-input challenges. Analysis showed that most urinary cells were recipient-derived, indicating that urine primarily reflects the host immune environment rather than the graft parenchyma.
