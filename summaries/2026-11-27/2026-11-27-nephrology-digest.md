### Abstract

Ambient air pollution may worsen outcomes among patients with organ failure awaiting transplant. We evaluated associations between residential ambient air pollution exposure and death or deterioration among United States transplant candidates. This retrospective cohort study used Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients data from 2005-2025, including heart, kidney, liver, and lung candidates in the United States linked to ZIP Code-level pollutant estimates. Exposures were particulate matter (PM2.5), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), and ozone (O3) before listing and during waitlist follow-up.

### Abstract

Steatotic donor livers are vulnerable to cold preservation-associated ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI), but the upstream mechanism is unclear. We investigated whether the m6A demethylase FTO regulates cold-sensitive TRPM8 signaling in steatotic liver grafts using human biopsies, diet-induced murine steatotic grafts, multi-omics, RNA stability assays, and hepatocyte-specific genetic models. FTO was upregulated in steatotic grafts and correlated with early post-transplant injury. FTO bound, demethylated, and stabilized TRPM8 mRNA, increasing TRPM8 expression.

## 4. The allograft survival of kidneys from living donors ≥65 years is similar to that of younger living donors in recipients ≥35 years

- Journal: AJT
- Publication date: 2026-11-26T00:00:00+00:00

- URL: [Article link](https://www.amjtransplant.org/article/S1600-6135(26)02848-0/fulltext?rss=yes)
- Matched keywords: kidney, dialysis, transplant

### Abstract

Concerns about kidney longevity may limit the use of living donors ≥65 years. Previous studies have not considered competing risks of death or effect modification by recipient age. We studied 106 966 living donor recipients in the Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients between 2000 and 2020, including 2376 with donors ≥65 years, for the outcome of allograft failure defined by return to dialysis or repeat transplantation. In a multivariable model accounting for the competing risk of death, the impact of donor age ≥65 years varied by recipient age: compared with recipients < 35 years with donors < 35 years, the subdistribution hazard ratios (SHRs) for allograft failure in recipients of donors ≥65 years were SHR = 1.77, 95% confidence interval (CI), 1.15 to 2.27 among recipients < 35 years; SHR = 1.14, 95% CI, 0.6 to 2.18 among recipients 35 to 49 years; SHR = 0.72, 95% CI, 0.38 to 1.39 among recipients 50 to 64 years; and SHR = 0.65, 95% CI, 0.33 to 1.29 among recipients ≥65 years.
