### Abstract

The field of transplant biomarkers is undergoing a necessary and overdue maturation. The initial era, defined by discovery-driven enthusiasm and proliferation of candidate markers, is giving way to a more disciplined phase, where the central questions are no longer what can be measured, but rather what these measurements mean biologically and how they should be positioned within clinical decision-making frameworks.1 The key issue is not incremental improvements in diagnosis, but whether the field can move from a collection of biomarkers to an articulated, biology-based context for interpreting allograft injury.
