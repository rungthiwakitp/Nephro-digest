If supportive care including transfusions and dialysis is available, most patients recover (Figure 1). Sethi and colleagues show that the recent decline in drug overdose mortality, a public-health success, coincided with a 45.1% decline in overdose decedent donor (ODD) kidney transplant rates, a plateau in overall deceased donor kidney transplantation, and reversal of the previously declining active kidney waitlist.1 The decline therefore acts as a stress test of kidney utilization infrastructure: it exposed a kidney-specific vulnerability to donor-pool shifts. Unlike liver transplantation, where increased non-ODD use preserved overall activity, deceased donor kidney transplantation did not compensate.
