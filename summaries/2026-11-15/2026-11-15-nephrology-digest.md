### Abstract

Anti-nephrin antibodies have been implicated in the pathogenesis of autoimmune podocytopathies, yet their prevalence and predictive value for post-transplant recurrence remain poorly defined. We conducted a retrospective multicenter cohort study through the TANGO Consortium, screening 49,536 kidney transplant recipients across 23 international centers and enrolling 136 patients with biopsy-proven primary FSGS and available pre-transplant serum samples. Circulating anti-nephrin IgG levels were measured by ELISA and validated against an immunoprecipitation assay in a nested cohort.
