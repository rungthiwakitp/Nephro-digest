### Abstract

Alpha-gal syndrome (AGS) is an IgE-mediated hypersensitivity to the oligosaccharide galactose-α-1,3-galactose, acquired through tick bites and increasingly recognized as a cause of allergic reactions to mammalian-derived medical products. Every major transplant modality creates distinct conditions under which sensitized patients may encounter alpha-gal, yet AGS knowledge has developed almost entirely outside the transplant literature. In conventional solid organ transplantation, risk arises primarily from perioperative exposures to porcine-derived heparin, gelatin-based hemostatic agents, and other mammalian-derived materials.

### Abstract

Simultaneous pancreas-kidney transplantation (SPKT) remains a highly effective treatment for patients with insulin-dependent diabetes and kidney failure. However, the use of pancreas transplants is limited by conservative donor selection and high rates of organ decline.1–3 Donor acute kidney injury (AKI) is a frequent reason for refusal, as elevated terminal creatinine (Cr) is often interpreted as an indicator of poor organ quality and increased technical risk.4–6 In practice, this often results in the rejection of donors who could have provided suitable pancreas and kidney allografts.

### Abstract

Operational tolerance after intestinal transplantation is exceptionally rare, with few reported cases and limited mechanistic understanding. We describe a 12-year-old girl who underwent multivisceral transplantation and subsequently developed graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) followed by Epstein–Barr virus–associated post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease (PTLD). Due to severe PTLD complications, immunosuppression was withdrawn 8 months post-transplant. The patient achieved viral remission and has remained free of rejection for more than three years without immunosuppressive therapy.
