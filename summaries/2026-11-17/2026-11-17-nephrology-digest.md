### Abstract

Alport syndrome (AS) is the second most common genetic kidney disease. It is an inherited disorder of type IV collagen, the main structural component of the glomerular basement membrane (GBM), caused by pathogenic variants in the genes COL4A3, COL4A4, and COL4A5 encoding the alpha 3, alpha 4 or alpha 5 collagen IV chains (α3/α4/α5[IV]). This leads to GBM defects, clinically presenting with microscopic hematuria and/or proteinuria and is variably associated with sensorineural hearing loss and ophthalmological defects.

### Abstract

Acute lung allograft dysfunction (ALAD) was recently defined by ISHLT consensus. Its severity scale has not been evaluated against outcomes, and the contributions of etiology and alloimmune injury are uncertain.This retrospective single-centre cohort included recipients transplanted between 2018 and 2024 surviving ≥30 days. Every post-transplant visit was reviewed; episodes were adjudicated for etiologies and graded on the ISHLT severity scale and a pre-specified six-point scale. Graft loss within 90 days was modelled by landmark Cox regression; graft loss and chronic lung allograft dysfunction (CLAD) over the whole follow-up by time-dependent Cox models.

### Abstract

Hepatitis A is an acute viral infection of the liver caused by the hepatitis A virus (HAV) and transmitted primarily through the fecal-oral route via contaminated food or water. Waterborne and foodborne outbreaks are most common, particularly in regions with inadequate sanitation infrastructure. The average incubation period for HAV is 28 days (range, 15-50 days). Although HAV infection is typically self-limiting in immunocompetent individuals, it can be significantly more severe in immunocompromised patients, including solid organ transplant recipients, and in those with chronic liver disease.
