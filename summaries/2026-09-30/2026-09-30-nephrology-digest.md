### Abstract

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) is an aggressive liver cancer with limited curative options. Deceased donor liver transplant is associated with survival benefit in select patients with unresectable iCCA, although outcomes are limited by high waitlist mortality and use of expanded criteria grafts, given ongoing deceased donor scarcity. This retrospective, single-center study investigated outcomes for seven patients who underwent living donor liver transplant (LDLT) for unresectable but liver-confined iCCA, managed under a transplant oncology protocol.
