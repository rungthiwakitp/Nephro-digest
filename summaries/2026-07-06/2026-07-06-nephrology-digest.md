- URL: [Article link](https://www.kidney-international.org/article/S0085-2538(26)00502-8/fulltext?rss=yes)
- Matched keywords: kidney, glomerular, IgA nephropathy

### Abstract

In the widely accepted “four-hit hypothesis” of how IgA nephropathy (IgAN) develops, elevated production of galactose-deficient (Gd)-IgA1 constitutes the first pathogenic step, followed by the generation of IgG or IgA autoantibodies against Gd-IgA1, formation of circulating immune complexes and mesangial deposition, and finally glomerular injury and scarring. In the current issue of Kidney International, the role of Gd-IgA1 in the pathogenesis of IgAN is discussed from 2 different perspectives. The “pro” article by Hitoshi Suzuki and Jan Novak argues that Gd-IgA1 is indeed the principal autoantigen driving disease (1), whereas the “con” article by Jicheng Lv and colleagues contends that Gd-IgA1 alone is insufficient to trigger the onset of IgAN and may represent only a biomarker or one component of a broader pathogenic process (2).

### Abstract

IgA nephropathy (IgAN), the most common primary glomerulonephritis, is characterized by mesangial deposition of IgA-containing immune complexes, predominantly IgA1. Galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) has long been proposed as the initiating molecule in the “4-hit” hypothesis of disease pathogenesis: overproduction of Gd-IgA1 (hit 1) triggers the production of Gd-IgA1–specific autoantibodies (hit 2), leading to immune complex formation (hit 3) and subsequent mesangial deposition with glomerular inflammation (hit 4).
